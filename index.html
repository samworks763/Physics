Step 1: Breaking Disk into Thin Rings

Instead of looking at the full solid plate at once, we divide the disk into concentric circular rings. For a ring of radius $r$ and thickness $dr$, the differential charge $dq$ is calculated using surface charge density $\sigma$.

$$dq = \sigma dA = \sigma (2\pi r \, dr)$$

Step 2: Electric Field of a Charged Ring

Using the standard formula for electric field due to a ring along its axis at distance $z$, we substitute $dq$ into the field expression.

$$dE = \frac{k \cdot dq \cdot z}{(z^2 + r^2)^{3/2}} = \frac{k \cdot (\sigma 2\pi r \, dr) \cdot z}{(z^2 + r^2)^{3/2}}$$

Step 3: Integration from 0 to Radius $a$

To find the total electric field for the entire disk, we integrate $dE$ from $r = 0$ to $r = a$ (disk radius). Constants are factored outside.

$$E = k \sigma (2\pi) z \int_{0}^{a} \frac{r \, dr}{(z^2 + r^2)^{3/2}}$$

Step 4 & 5: Substitution & Final Result

Let $t = z^2 + r^2 \implies r \, dr = dt / 2$. Solving the integral and applying limits yields the exact standard disk formula.

$$\vec{E} = \frac{\sigma}{2\varepsilon_0} \left[ 1 - \frac{z}{\sqrt{z^2 + a^2}} \right] \hat{z}$$
